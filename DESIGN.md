# Anton CGI Design Context

## Direction

Engineering, craft, and side projects for fellow makers. Combine Athos's editorial type, substantial skill chips, and overlapping projects with Anton's established midnight/peach palette, Alpine Dawn ASCII scene, and full-width content surface. Keep copy concise and omit sales, hiring, invented metrics, and unnecessary controls.

## Color

Keep the scene's deepest midnight tone (#090c18), off-white (#e9eaf2), blue-gray (#aeb6cc), and dawn-peach (#f2b6a0). Green is outside Anton's UI style. Both featured cards use one continuous #12151f surface; #1a1e2b is reserved for skill chips and pressed states. Do not put a separate background behind the project text. Photographs and the existing cream frames supply visual variety. Keep visible keyboard focus.

## Typography

Use local Satoshi, with Bold for the name and section/project headings, Regular for prose, and Medium for small project names. Open with “Hi, I’m” above a prominent but restrained “Anton CGI” name, followed by “I design and build software.” Use a fluid name size of about 42–96px, section headings of 28–36px, skill chips of 16–19px, body copy around 16–18px, and project descriptions at 18–22px. Tracking stops at -.04em. Let the name wrap when text is enlarged; never force it outside the viewport. Retain one main heading and exactly one hero subtitle. Employer, location, and independent-app details belong in About.

## Motion

Use ascii.rest's Alpine Dawn on a fixed, full-viewport dark stage. On every screen, the background scene is 80vh tall and centered vertically at 50vh; the hero remains 100vh. Preserve the scene’s 2:1 aspect ratio. Center the scene horizontally on landscape screens; on portrait screens crop the sides and frame the main mountain around 38% of its width. Preserve the scene’s proportions, never stretch it. Fade the top and bottom over 4% and both side edges over 10% of the scene, intersecting the masks so the art blends into the page on every edge. Keep the prefixed mask composition for Safari. Keep the scene fully visible in the hero. Below it, transition vertically into a full-width midnight ground matching the scene (#090c18), so the work has a quiet, readable backdrop. Avoid vertical seams and narrow reading-column effects. Sample the original geometry at 400×200 characters (four times the original detail), with a matching static SVG fallback. Host the MIT-licensed source locally. Compute the scene in a worker at up to 15fps and paint with ordinary Canvas 2D for Chrome and Safari compatibility. If worker startup fails or frames stall, continue with the main-thread renderer. Never transfer the visible canvas. Load the committed classic-script bundle so direct file previews also animate; regenerate it with `node scripts/build-alpine.mjs` after editing the scene, renderer, or controller. Run the cloud, mist, and water cycle at three times the original pace so motion is perceptible. Pause in hidden tabs and hold still by default for reduced motion. Keep Play/Pause out of the visible layout. Retain a keyboard-accessible control that appears only when focused and remembers an explicit playback choice for the current tab session. Move the tool strip with a slow CSS transform; pause it on hover and with the background pause preference. For reduced motion, show one static wrapping group with every tool visible. Keep the short CSS entrance and subtle hover transitions. The homepage has its own `assets/css/portfolio.css`; do not change shared app-page styles for homepage work.

Its tiny captions and sequential guides are artwork; platform labels outside the artwork remain at least 14px on mobile.
